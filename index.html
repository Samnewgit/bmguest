The $500,000 Capital Allocation Dilemma

You are the analyst. Ten weeks of evidence. One recommendation.

Available capital
$500,000

Which branch should receive the investment?

Your recommendation must survive a boardroom question: “What evidence makes this choice better?”

Do not choose the branch with the biggest number. Choose the branch whose statistical profile best fits the objective.
Branch A

Stable performer

  • Revenue changes are relatively smooth.
  • Advertising response appears predictable.
  • Lower operational uncertainty.
Branch B

Volatile performer

  • Large swings between weeks.
  • Higher possible revenue upside.
  • Greater uncertainty around planning.

Regression: Does Advertising Spend Move With Revenue?

A regression line is not just a formula. It is a compact picture of direction, rate of change and prediction quality.

Interpretation: stronger slope, but more scatter → higher prediction error.

Extrapolation: A Model Can Become Dangerous Outside Its Evidence

At $18k advertising spend, Branch B looks dramatically better — but the prediction is outside the observed range.

Observed region vs projected region
Tempting conclusion
92.85k
Projected Branch B revenue at $18k ad spend.

The model has crossed the evidence boundary.

Past linear behaviour does not guarantee future linear behaviour. Saturation, capacity limits, competition and diminishing returns may break the pattern.

Prediction confidence should decrease as the model moves further away from the data used to build it.
